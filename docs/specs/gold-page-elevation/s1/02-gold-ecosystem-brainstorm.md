Market observations quoted here were taken from the audited pages on
16–17 Nov 2026 (spot ≈ $3,857–3,880, ~31% below the late-Jan 2026 ATH of
≈ $5,590–5,710 depending on source; CPI 4.0%; 10y real ≈ 2.9%; DXY

## A. Monetary policy & interest rates

**Mechanism.** Gold pays no coupon and has no cash flow, so its main
financial competitor is the risk-free real yield. But the transmission
is via *expectations*, not the level of rates: a hike that is fully
priced does nothing; a dovish surprise inside a hiking cycle can lift
gold. The decomposition matters more than the headline: nominal yield =
real yield + breakeven inflation (+ term premium). High nominal yields
driven by high *breakevens* are gold-positive; high nominal yields
driven by surging *real* yields are gold-negative; high yields driven by
*term premium* (fiscal/credibility risk) can be gold-positive (see C and
the S1/03 chains 1, 3).

**What to watch.** 10y TIPS real yield (the single most-cited driver);
2y yield as the policy-expectations proxy; the Fed path priced by
markets (futures/prediction markets); QT pace (liquidity); policy
divergence (Fed vs ECB/BoJ — moves USD/JPY and the dollar lane).

**Signal quality & horizon.** Real yields: strong cyclical relationship
pre-2022, visibly weakened after 2022 when official-sector buying became
the marginal bid (both facts are chartable; the break itself is the
lesson). Expectations surprises: days-to-weeks horizon, powerful but
event-driven. QT/liquidity: contested, long-lag claims — treat as
Advanced/explanatory, not a dashboard number.

**Verdict.** Core (real yields, policy-expectations card). Currena
already has 7 borrowing-cost voters incl. 5y real, 30y nominal, ECB rate
— the data is there; what is missing is the *narrative decomposition*
(the "why yields up ≠ gold down" explainer, S1/03 chain 3).

## B. Bond market & fixed-income dynamics

**Mechanism.** Three distinct stories hide inside "yields rose":
(i) higher expected real growth (bearish gold), (ii) higher expected
inflation (neutral-to-bullish gold), (iii) higher term premium —
investors demanding compensation for fiscal/supply/credibility risk
(bullish gold, because the same fear that lifts term premium lifts
debasement hedges). The 2000s-2010s "real yields rule" assumed (iii) was
flat; 2022–2026 broke that assumption. Bond-market *stress* (vault
raids, liquidity gaps, failed auctions) is a different channel again:
flight-to-safety can initially favor Treasuries *and* gold.

**What to watch.** Term premium estimates (ACM — Currena already has a
voter), breakevens (1y, 5y, 5y5y — present), MOVE index (bond vol),
Treasury auction tails, debt/GDP and the 12m deficit (Currena has the
deficit voter).

**Verdict.** Supporting (breakevens, term premium exist as voters);
Advanced (auctions, MOVE). The page-level gap is not new data, it is the
*yield-decomposition card* that makes the existing data legible
(S1/04 Idea 5).

## C. Inflation & monetary credibility

**Mechanism.** Gold's inflation story is horizon-dependent: over
quarters, real yields dominate (high inflation with hawkish response =
real yields up = gold pressured); over decades, gold tracks purchasing
power (the 1970s and the 2020s are the proof cases). The *gap* between
current inflation and market-expected future inflation (breakevens) is
the cleanest single summary: CPI above breakevens = markets believe
disinflation is coming (today: 4.0% vs 2.2% — exactly the current
setup); that gap closing via hawkish policy is the gold-negative path;
the gap widening via credibility loss is the gold-positive path.

**What to watch.** Headline/core CPI and PCE (present as voters),
5y5y breakeven (present — the "long-run credibility" number), the
CPI-vs-breakeven gap (computable from existing series — new derived
card), sticky-price CPI (not present; Advanced).

**Verdict.** Core for the credibility framing, Supporting for the raw
indices. The differentiation is again narrative: no Persian page
currently explains the *gap* — Saham Analiz explains the pieces well but
does not name the credibility channel.

## D. Currency markets

**Mechanism.** Gold is priced in USD, so dollar moves are a valuation
arithmetic (a stronger dollar mechanically raises gold for non-USD
buyers, dampening their demand) — but the correlation is regime-dependent
and breaks when the dollar rises *because of* stress (both bid) or when
de-dollarization flows dominate (both rise 2024–25 episodes). Gold in
non-USD terms (EUR, JPY, CNY) is the honest way to show a Persian/global
audience that "gold's bear market" is sometimes only a dollar story.
Currena already shows gold in AUD and the broad/EME dollar indexes.

**What to watch.** DXY (present), broad dollar index (present — better
constructed), USD/JPY + US–JP 10y gap (present), reserve-share metrics
(present — the de-dollarization slow gauge). Advanced: gold-in-X-currency
grid with local ATH flags.

**Verdict.** Core (dollar lane as-is), Supporting (multi-currency gold
grid — cheap, high educational value).

## E. Central banks & institutional demand

**Mechanism.** The structural story of the decade: after the 2022
freezing of Russia's FX reserves, official-sector buying roughly doubled
(WGC-reported ~1,000t/yr pace through 2025-26; benchmark report S1/08
notes 321t in Q3'26 alone per WGC). This demand is comparatively
price-insensitive, which raises the floor and weakened the old
real-yields relationship. It is *the* candidate explanation for the
2022+ correlation break the page should teach. Note the distinction:
central-bank *announcements/surveys* are slow structural data; *reported
purchases* are monthly; Chinese PBoC additions are disclosed
irregularly with long lags.

**What to watch.** WGC quarterly demand data (LICENCE-GATED for
Currena — the page's own disclosure says WGC requires prior written
authorisation; the owner's lever, see S1/09); IMF IFS monthly reserves
(L1-licence, already a repo source); ETF flows monthly (WGC-gated; ETF
holdings via fund pages are L2-fail-closed today); LBMA vault holdings
(free, monthly, licence-clean per S1/08).

**Verdict.** The biggest *content* gap on the page today — the Flows &
demand lane has 2 of 37 voters, both COT. Fixing this is data-politics
before it is data-engineering: the WGC permission letter is the single
highest-leverage owner action identified in S1.

## F. Geopolitical & financial risk

**Mechanism.** Two channels that must not be conflated: (i) the
*headline premium* — war/shock bids that decay in days-to-weeks when the
supply/flow consequences don't materialize (Ukraine 2022: spike, full
round-trip); (ii) the *structural channel* — sanctions and reserve
freezes change official-sector behavior semi-permanently (the 2022
freeze is what doubled CB buying). The page already carries GPR (7-day),
EPU, STLFSI, NFCI, VIX, bank-stress voters — good coverage of (i)'s
financial side.

**Verdict.** Supporting (already strong). The elevation is the
education: an "episode card" pattern that shows what each stress metric
did in past shocks (S1/04 Idea 4 covers the interactive version).

## G. Gold supply & physical demand

**Mechanism.** Supply is inelastic and boring — that is the point. Mine
production (~3,600t/yr, per WGC via benchmarks) responds to price over
5–10 year cycles; recycling is the only flexible supply source and is
price-responsive. Demand is where the variance is: jewelry (price-
sensitive, especially India/China), bar & coin (retail investment),
technology (small), OTC & investment flows. Regional physical premiums
(Shanghai vs London) are the market's own "where is the marginal buyer"
gauge — Currena already shows the Shanghai premium (0.47% at audit) and
SGE withdrawals and Swiss imports (both present). This lane is quietly
excellent and differentiated already.

**Verdict.** Supporting (present). Advanced additions: India premium,
LBMA vault series (free), recycling share. AISC cost-curve (WGC) only if
the licence lands.

## H. Futures, positioning & market structure

**Mechanism.** Positioning (CFTC CoT managed money) is a *positioning*
indicator, not a driver: at extremes it marks crowded trades (contrarian
value at 1-year-high/low tails), in trends it confirms momentum. Current
audit data: net spec long 112k contracts mid-range for gold, silver 3.6k
near 1-year lows — the "crowded short in silver" reading Saham Analiz
also surfaces. Futures curve structure (contango/backwardation), COMEX
registered inventory, and EFP spreads are market-integrity gauges —
the 2025 tariff-scare gold-flights-to-NY episode is the case study.

**Verdict.** Core-lite: COT is present (2 voters + cards); add the
z-score/percentile framing and the extremes interpretation. Market
structure (EFP, inventories) is Advanced — interesting, but data access
(COMEX data is scrape-hostile per S1/08 CME finding) and audience fit
argue for education-only treatment, not live data.

## I. Technical & quantitative analysis

**Mechanism (and honest limits).** Technicals summarize the price's own
history: trend (50/200DMA), overbought/oversold (RSI — misleading in
strong trends; can ride >70 for months), volatility (realized), and
drawdown. For a conditions page, two derived stats carry most of the
value: *distance from 200DMA* (regime + mean-reversion context — current
−13.6% below 200DMA per the Saham Analiz chart readout) and *position in
10-year range* (Currena already does this natively — "top 11% of the
last 10 years"). Full TA suites (MACD, Bollinger, pivots ×5 methods)
are what the Persian market *expects* (TGJU pattern, S1/08) but they
would pull Currena toward the crowded "technical terminal" position and
away from its differentiated "conditions + causes" identity.

**Verdict.** Supporting: keep and extend Currena's statistical framing
(range percentiles, 30-day speed — already present). Omit for now:
oscillator suites (deliberate exclusion; revisit only with evidence of
user demand; see S1/05 §7).

## J. Cross-asset relationships

**What the ratios actually tell you.** Gold/silver 82 vs 20y-average 71
(monetary vs industrial demand mix — silver cheap vs gold); gold/BTC
(the debasement-trade rivalry — BTC per ounce 0.0598 at audit);
gold/oil 40.9 (energy-purchasing power); gold/SPY (0.47, long-cycle
relative value); real gold price CPI-deflated $1,155 — "top 10% of the
last 10 years" (the long-horizon value gauge that no Persian competitor
shows; Currena already has it). Redundancy check: gold/oil and
gold/copper overlap (both = gold vs industrial cycle); keep both but
group them. Gold vs bonds is the real-yields card restated — show as
one thing, not two.

**Verdict.** Supporting (all present as ratio cards — this is already a
strength). Elevation: pair each ratio card with a one-line "what this
ratio means" and its historical range context (silver ratio has this on
Saham Analiz; Currena's cards have the decile but not the plain meaning).
