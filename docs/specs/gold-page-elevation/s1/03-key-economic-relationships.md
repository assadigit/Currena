# S1/03 — The Key Economic Relationships a Gold Page Must Explain

*The causal chains, with mechanism, needed indicators, known exceptions,
horizon, and how Currena could communicate each visually. This is the
seed of the S7 analytical-framework design; chain numbers are stable
identifiers for later sessions.*

## Chain 1 — The opportunity-cost chain (the classic, and its break)

```
Fed policy expectations → nominal Treasury yields → (minus breakevens)
→ REAL yields → opportunity cost of a zero-yield asset → Western
investment demand (ETFs/futures) → gold price
```

- **Mechanism.** Every dollar in gold forgoes the risk-free real return;
  when that return rises, financial gold demand falls, and vice versa.
- **Indicators.** 10y TIPS real yield (core), 2y nominal (expectations
  proxy), breakevens (the bridge), ETF flows (the transmission
  observable).
- **Exceptions — the essential lesson.** (i) Expectations vs level: a
  priced-in hike is a non-event; a dovish surprise in a hiking cycle can
  rally gold. (ii) The 2022+ break: real yields rose from −1% to ~+2.9%
  while gold *rose* to repeated ATHs — because the marginal buyer
  rotated from yield-sensitive Western investors to comparatively
  price-insensitive central banks (Chains 4/5). The correlation has
  visibly weakened (Saham Analiz computes weekly-correlation −0.13 over
  its chart window and attributes the weakening to CB buying — a rare
  honest treatment S1/07).
- **Horizon.** Cyclical (quarters), with event-level (days) surprises.
- **Visual treatment.** Dual-axis chart gold vs 10y real, with rolling
  correlation strip below and an annotated "2022: the buyer rotated"
  marker. Currena has both series already; this is assembly + education,
  not new data.

## Chain 2 — The dollar chain (arithmetic, not destiny)

```
Dollar supply/demand (rates divergence, risk flows, USD funding)
→ DXY/broad dollar → gold price in non-USD buyers' hands → physical &
investment demand outside the US → gold price in USD
```

- **Mechanism.** Valuation arithmetic plus demand elasticity. A stronger
  dollar makes gold costlier in EUR/JPY/CNY/IRR, damping non-US demand.
- **Exceptions.** (i) Stress regimes: dollar and gold bid together
  (both are the same flight-to-safety trade). (ii) De-dollarization
  regimes: reserve diversification lifts gold *because* the dollar's
  role is questioned — both rise. (iii) Gold in other currencies can sit
  at all-time highs while the USD price consolidates (2023 EUR-gold
  ATHs vs USD-gold range — the multi-currency grid makes this visible).
- **Horizon.** Cyclical; structural in the reserve-share tail.
- **Visual.** Currena's dollar lane exists; the addition is a small
  multi-currency gold grid (EUR, JPY, GBP, CNY, AUD, IRR-if-approved)
  with local 10-year decile flags.

## Chain 3 — The term-premium / credibility chain (why yields up ≠ gold down)

```
Fiscal deficits & debt supply → sovereign-credibility concern → TERM
PREMIUM rises → nominal yields rise (while real yields stagnate) →
debasement-hedge demand (gold, hard assets) rises → gold price
```

- **Mechanism.** When yields rise because lenders demand risk
  compensation (not because real growth or policy rates rose), the same
  fear that lifts term premium lifts gold. This is the cleanest answer
  to the most common reader question of 2025–26 ("yields are 5%, why is
  gold not collapsing?").
- **Indicators.** ACM 10y term premium (already a Currena voter), US
  12m deficit (present), debt/GDP, 5y5y breakeven (present).
- **Exceptions.** Term-premium spikes inside funding stress can
  initially hit gold via margin/liquidity spirals (2022 gilt-episode
  analog) before the hedge bid dominates.
- **Horizon.** Structural (years) — this is the chain that explains the
  decade, not the week.
- **Visual.** A yield-decomposition card: nominal = real + breakeven +
  term premium, three small stacked/bar components with "which piece
  moved this month" and what that combination has historically meant for
  gold. Nobody in Persian does this; few do it in English.

## Chain 4 — The marginal-buyer chain (the 2022 rotation)

```
Sanctions/reserve-freeze risk → official-sector reassessment → central
bank purchases (~1,000t/yr since 2022 vs ~500t before) + Asian retail
bar&coin → price-insensitive bid raises the floor → gold decouples from
real yields → Western investors re-enter later via ETFs (2025 flow
return) → momentum completes the move
```

- **Mechanism.** Price is set at the margin; when the marginal buyer's
  motivation shifts from opportunity cost to reserve safety, the old
  elasticities weaken. The freeze of ~$300bn of Russian reserves in 2022
  is the canonical trigger.
- **Indicators.** WGC quarterly CB purchases (LICENCE-GATED — the
  owner's permission letter, S1/09), IMF IFS monthly reserves (L1,
  available), gold ETF holdings/flows (gated), SGE withdrawals +
  Shanghai premium (present), LBMA vault holdings (free, monthly).
- **Exceptions.** CB buying does not put a floor under every drawdown —
  it is slow, lumpy, and reported with lags; it explains the *floor
  rising over quarters*, not weekly support. Turkish/Russian purchases
  are partly local-currency/geo-political specific.
- **Horizon.** Structural.
- **Visual.** A "who is buying gold" stacked view: quarterly demand by
  segment (CB / ETF / bar&coin / jewelry / tech) — the single most
  powerful chart in institutional gold research (WGC Gold Demand
  Trends), currently absent from the Persian web entirely because of the
  data-licence wall. Behind the WGC permission lever.

## Chain 5 — The inflation-vs-credibility gap chain

```
Inflation print vs market-expected future inflation (breakevens) → if
print > expectations persistently → policy forced hawkish (real yields
up) OR credibility questioned (term premium up) → first path pressures
gold, second supports it → watch which channel the market chooses
```

- **Mechanism.** The *gap*, not the level, carries information: today's
  4.0% CPI vs 2.2% 10y breakeven says markets still trust disinflation
  (via the ~90%-priced Dec hike). If breakevens start rising toward the
  print instead, credibility is cracking — historically the
  gold-explosive configuration (1970s redux).
- **Indicators.** CPI (present), 5y and 5y5y breakevens (present), the
  derived gap (new, computable from existing series).
- **Exceptions.** One-month gaps are noise (shutdown-distorted Oct 2025
  data is a live example of data-gap honesty, handled well by the
  competitor); the chain matters on 3–6 month persistence.
- **Horizon.** Cyclical-to-structural bridge.
- **Visual.** The gap chart (CPI line vs breakeven line with shaded
  regime) + plain-language reading. Cheap, differentiated, educational.

## Chain 6 — The positioning chain (fuel, not driver)

```
Price trend → spec capital chases (managed-money longs build) → at
extremes the trade is crowded → marginal buyer exhausted → mean
reversion risk ↑ (silver today: 3.6k net long, near 1y lows = crowded
bearishness, fuel for squeezes)
```

- **Mechanism.** Positioning is the market's leverage/fuel gauge. Extremes
  mark vulnerability (either direction); mid-range readings carry little
  information — which is exactly what "in the middle of its 1-year range"
  should teach the reader.
- **Indicators.** COT managed money net + gross longs (present as 2
  voters and 2 cards), z-scored vs 10y (the elevation — Currena already
  computes percentile10y for other series).
- **Horizon.** Short-term (days–weeks).
- **Visual.** Existing positioning bars + a 10-year percentile strip and
  an "extreme" flag with plain interpretation text.

## Chain 7 — The physical-flow chain (Asia premium signal)

```
Regional physical demand (China/India) ↔ London/COMEX paper price →
Shanghai premium/discount → when premium persists, physical demand is
the marginal bid (and arb flows metal east) → support independent of
Western financial demand
```

- **Mechanism.** The premium is the market's own sensor for where the
  marginal physical buyer is. Persistent premiums (2023–24 China) marked
  the rotation Chain 4 describes; discounts mark Western-led phases.
- **Indicators.** Shanghai premium (present, 0.47% at audit), SGE
  withdrawals (present), Swiss imports (present — the Europe→Asia
  pipeline), India premium (absent; WGC-gated).
- **Horizon.** Cyclical.
- **Visual.** Already strong; group the three cards under a "physical
  market" mini-header with a shared reading.

## Chain 8 — The Iran bridge chain (the unowned Persian opportunity)

```
Global ounce (USD) × USD/IRR rate (free/NIMA) → imported-gold base
price → + domestic premiums/distress → mesghal / 18k / Emami coin
price → coin premium (حباب) = local fear & liquidity gauge
```

- **Mechanism.** Iranian gold = global gold + FX + local premium. The
  decomposition answers the single most-asked question in the Persian
  market ("why did the coin move when the ounce didn't?") — and nobody
  computes it in one view (S1/08 gap analysis; TGJU shows prices, not
  decomposition; Saham Analiz has a USD-only jewelry calculator).
- **Indicators needed.** Ounce (present), USD/IRR free-market rate
  (new source — licence/cost TBD), mesghal/18k/coin quotes (new source),
  historical coin premium series (buildable from own history).
- **Exceptions/risks.** Local price controls, import rules, and
  rial/toman duality create real UX decisions; domestic data quality and
  licensing need S4 verification. This is also a *positioning* decision:
  it anchors Currena's identity to the Iranian user in a way the current
  global-only stance avoids. Owner decision required (CP-A).
- **Horizon.** All three at once — that's the point.
- **Visual.** A decomposition strip: "today's coin move: X% ounce, Y%
  dollar, Z% premium" — the KGX-style logic (S1/08) applied to Tehran.
