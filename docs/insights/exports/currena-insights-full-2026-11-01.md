| Real yields and gold | Differs from the usual pattern | Real yields and gold usually move in opposite directions: when “safe” money pays more after inflation, gold tends to lose shine, and the other way round. — real yields: falling · gold: falling |
| The dollar, gold and oil | Trigger not present | Gold and oil are priced in US dollars, so a strong dollar tends to weigh on them, and a weak one tends to lift them. — the dollar: rising · gold: falling · oil: rising |
| Oil, inflation expectations and borrowing costs | Trigger not present | When oil jumps, markets tend to expect more inflation, and long-term borrowing costs tend to rise with it. — oil: rising · inflation expectations: falling · 10-year borrowing costs: falling |
| Copper demand, Chinese factories and money | Not enough fresh data | Copper is wired into industry, so a firm copper-to-gold ratio tends to come with busy factories.
